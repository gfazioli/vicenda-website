# Settings

**⌘,** opens it, and the field at the top searches the panes. It matches the
words you would reach for as well as the ones printed on screen, so `sqlite`,
`snooze` or `dark` each land on the right row. A preference you cannot find is
one you do not have.

## Permissions — the ones that matter

Two switches decide whether Vicenda may change anything in your mailbox, and
both are off until you turn them on. They live in the **Accounts** pane.

**Allow mail modification** is the master, and it is a local switch rather than
a new permission. Vicenda asks Google for one permission when you connect an
account, and it is the narrowest one that can also mark a message read; this
switch decides whether Vicenda ever uses that half. Turning it on does not send
you back to a consent screen, and turning it off does not give the permission
back — it stops mail being changed on the server. It is a real decision, which
is why it is not bundled into the first launch.

**Mark read on the server** decides whether reading in Vicenda also marks read
in Gmail. It requires the switch above — without it, Vicenda's idea of read and
Gmail's drift apart, which is a worse outcome than not offering it at all.

## Accounts

**Connected mailboxes** lists what Vicenda is reading, each with the colour it
wears everywhere else in the app, and each saying in its own words what it can
actually do — read only, or read and write — rather than a green tick that
means nothing. Which colour a mailbox gets, and why the colours never move, is
on [Accounts and Colour](/docs/accounts).

**Add account** (**⇧⌘N** from anywhere) starts the consent flow in a system
sheet over the app. No browser opens, and Vicenda cannot see into the sheet.
The whole of the first connection is on
[Getting Started](/docs/getting-started#connecting-a-mailbox).

## The app itself

**Open Vicenda at login** does what it says, through the system's own
login-items mechanism.

**Check for updates automatically** is on by default and can be turned off.
Vicenda checks with the site it was downloaded from, downloads an update only
when you agree to it, and each build is signed and notarised. **Check for
updates** beside it asks now rather than waiting.

**Version** shows the version and the build number, which is the number to
quote if you ever report something.

## Appearance

**Theme** is Dark, Light or Match system. The whole palette is dynamic, so the
change is immediate and reaches windows that are already open. Dark is the
theme the app was born in; Match system follows macOS, automatic appearance
included.

One thing does not follow the theme: the **account colours**. A hue means a
mailbox, in any light. That rule is absolute and it is explained on
[Accounts and Colour](/docs/accounts).

## Messages

**Sender pictures** — three independent sources, each explained on the
[Privacy](/docs/privacy) page. The pane also tells you where the address-book
permission stands, because "no photo" was otherwise four different facts
wearing one face: never asked, refused, not in the address book, or the source
is off.

**Lines before the cut** — how much of a message a channel shows before
collapsing it. More lines make the column easier to read and the day longer to
scroll.

**Draw the stream as a document** — an alternative renderer for the message
stream. The classic list cannot measure a row it has not built, so it reserves
a fixed height and drops whatever does not fit the width; the document lays
everything out, so a card keeps every field and a narrow window makes the row
taller instead of shorter.

## The sidebar

The three knobs here decide whether the column is a **working set** or a file
manager. The defaults are drawn from a real mailbox rather than chosen round:
189 labels existed and 18 had seen traffic in a fortnight; 391 sending
addresses existed and 157 had.

- **Days of activity** that keep a label or an address in the column.
- **Rows per section** before "show all".
- **Only people you have written to** — see
  [Channels and Threads](/docs/the-stream) for why this is on.

Changing any of them rebuilds the column immediately.

## Reminders

The default offset for "remind me". Reminders never leave the Mac.

## Local Model

Optional, and off until you choose one. The pane lists the models Vicenda can
use with what each costs in download and in memory, downloads one, makes it the
one in use, or removes it. The files live outside the app, so an update never
downloads them again.

**Try it** picks a recent message from a sender no template claims and shows
the card the model would build, or says why it built none — which is how to
decide whether the download is worth it before committing to it.

What it adds, which two models are offered, and the rules that decide what it
is never shown, are on [The Local Model](/docs/local-model).

## Advanced

**Messages per page** when walking back up a channel.

**Check the store every** decides how often the local store is examined for
outside changes. Vicenda does not re-read your mail on a timer — it asks the
database for its own change counter, which costs 0.0017 ms, and does nothing
until something has actually been written. A tighter interval therefore costs
almost nothing.

**Where the store lives** prints the path of the database, ready to select and
copy. That one file holds the full text of every message Vicenda has
downloaded. It is created `0600` inside a `0700` folder, which means readable
by your user account and by nothing else.
