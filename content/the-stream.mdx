# Channels and Threads

This is the part of Vicenda that is not like other mail clients, so it is worth
a page of its own.

## Two populations, separated at the door

Mail arrives from two kinds of sender, and they want opposite things from you.

A **machine** — your bank, a deployment bot, a subscription renewal — writes on
its own schedule, expects nothing back, and is only interesting when you go
looking. That is a **channel**: a place you visit.

A **person** waiting for an answer is the opposite. They are few, they are
owed something, and the number of them can reach zero by the end of a day.
That is a **thread**, and it belongs in a list that empties.

Most clients put both in one column and give you filters to pull them apart
again. Vicenda separates them before you see them, which is why the sidebar has
two sections rather than one inbox and a set of rules.

<Callout type="info">
  The rule that decides which is which is deliberately strict: **a person is
  someone you have written to**. Without that, a payment notice, a taxi receipt
  and a phone bill all look like people — measured on a real mailbox, 28 of the
  42 senders once filed as people were machines. The switch is in **Settings →
  Sidebar** if you want the looser rule.
</Callout>

## The stream reads like a conversation

A channel opens as a **stream**: oldest at the top, newest at the bottom, and
it opens at the bottom — the shape of a place things arrive, rather than a list
of subjects you open one at a time.

Inside it:

- **Day dividers** break the run, and each one is also a jump: click it to go to
  today, yesterday, last week, or a date you pick.
- **A `New` line** marks where you stopped reading. It is drawn once, from what
  was unread when you opened the channel — so it survives the channel being
  marked read a second and a half later, which is when it would otherwise
  vanish under you.
- **Messages group into blocks** by sender *and subject*. Not by sender and time
  alone: six security advisories arriving in the same minute are six different
  things, and grouping them under one header would make them read as one.
- **`More unread above`** appears when the line you stopped at has scrolled off
  the top, and takes you back to it.

## Collapsed by default, and why

Every message shows its opening lines and says how many more there are.

That is not a space-saving default, it is what makes the shape survive contact
with real mail. Slack's layout works because Slack messages are short; email is
not. Measured on a real mailbox: one channel held 239 messages averaging 39,000
characters — about six hundred lines each, a hundred and forty thousand lines
in a single channel. Shown whole, it is not a stream, it is a wall.

Open a message and you get the real thing: the sender's own HTML, rendered
inline, with every remote resource blocked. See [Privacy](/docs/privacy).
