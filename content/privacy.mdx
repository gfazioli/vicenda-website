# Privacy

Vicenda reads your mail on your Mac and keeps it there. It runs no server, asks
you for no account, and sends no telemetry. This page says what it stores, every
request it makes, and what this website collects.

_Last updated: 29 October 2026._

## What stays on your Mac

Everything Vicenda knows about your mail is kept on your Mac:

- **your messages**: their headers (sender, recipients, subject, date), their
  bodies and the mailboxes or labels they sit in, as read from your provider;
- **your rules**: what you taught it with "from now on", for one account or for
  every account;
- **your decisions**: what you binned, kept under a label, put in To do or left
  for Later, and what you confirmed in a batch;
- **your accounts**: each one's address, server and colour.

They live in one database in a folder of your home directory,
`~/.vicenda-dump/`, which only your macOS user can open. Vicenda does not encrypt
it itself; with FileVault on, it is encrypted with the rest of your disk.

**The password of each account** is an app password, kept in the macOS login
**Keychain** under `com.gfazioli.Vicenda.imap` and never synchronised to iCloud.
It is not written into the database, into a preference or into a log.

## Your mailbox stays as it is

Today every decision you make in Vicenda is recorded **in Vicenda**, on your Mac.
Nothing is moved, archived or deleted on your provider's server: binning a mail
takes it out of your report, not out of your mailbox. Carrying decisions to the
server is still to come, and this page will say so when it does.

The one thing Vicenda can change on the server is whether a message is **read or
unread**, and only if you turn that on in Settings; it is off until you do.

## The model works on the device

Where Vicenda uses a model, to write a mail's one line in the report, it runs
on your Mac: **Apple Intelligence** on macOS 26 and later, or a **model you
download once** from Settings. No message, and
nothing about one, is sent anywhere to do it, and no message is used to train
anything.

## Every request Vicenda makes

These are all the connections the app opens. None of them carries your mail to
anybody but your own provider.

- **Your mail providers.** Vicenda connects to each account's IMAP server over
  an encrypted connection, at launch and then every few minutes, to read new
  mail. This is the only traffic that carries your mail, and it goes only
  between your Mac and your provider.
- **The model download**, only when you click Download in Settings: the files
  come from GitHub Releases and are checked against hashes built into the app.
  Apple Intelligence needs no download.
- **Update checks**, on unless you turn off "Check for updates automatically" in
  Settings: the app asks vicenda.app whether a new version exists. The request
  carries your IP address and the app's version.
- **Gravatar**, off unless you switch on "Ask Gravatar for the photo": the app
  sends `gravatar.com` a hash of a sender's address to ask for a photo. The
  address itself does not travel, but the hash identifies it, so Gravatar learns
  that this Mac corresponds with that person. The Mac's own address book, on by
  default, is the other source of photos, and nothing leaves the Mac for it.
- **The About window** shows a sponsor's picture, which is loaded from GitHub.

Two things Vicenda does **not** do today: it sends no mail of any kind, and it
sends no unsubscribe. "Bin and unsubscribe" bins the mail in Vicenda and records
your wish; the unsubscribe itself is not built yet. When it is, it will be a
single request to the address the sender published for that purpose, sent only
when you click or when a rule you taught says so.

## No pixel

Opening an email is the one everyday act that reports itself. A tracking pixel
is a tiny image on a sender's server: a client that fetches it to draw the
message tells the sender *this person opened it, at this time, from roughly
here*.

Vicenda does not make that request. Before a message is shown, every remote
resource in it is blocked, of every type, unconditionally. If that block cannot
be put in place, the message is shown as plain text instead, which cannot fetch
anything. JavaScript in a message is off, and a link you click opens in your
browser, not inside Vicenda.

## Removing your data

Removing an account in Vicenda deletes its password from the Keychain and its
mail from the local database, and changes nothing on the server. Deleting the `~/.vicenda-dump/` folder removes everything at once. A
downloaded model stays in `~/Library/Application Support/Vicenda/Models`, and
sender photos in the app's cache, until you delete them.

## This website

- **No cookies, no cross-site tracking.** The site sets no cookies of its own and does not follow you across other sites.
- **Visit statistics.** Visits are counted by privacy-friendly analytics that
  use no cookies and build no profile of you: pages viewed, the referring site, country, and browser or device type, in aggregate. Visits are grouped by a hash of the request that is discarded after 24 hours.
- **Server logs.** The hosting provider that serves these pages, and the app's
  update checks, processes your IP address and the details of each request to deliver the site and keep it secure; the request logs we can see are kept for one day.
- **Your browser's local storage** remembers, in your browser only, that you
  closed the newsletter prompt. None of it is sent to us.
- **Email.** If you write to us, we use your address and message only to answer
  you, and keep them no longer than the exchange needs.
- **GitHub.** The sponsor pictures at the bottom of every page are loaded from
  GitHub, which therefore sees your IP address. The app's model downloads also
  come from GitHub.
- **Pictures of the app.** The mail drawn on this site is invented: no sender,
  subject or address on it belongs to anybody.
- **Links.** The newsletter lives on Substack, sponsorships go through GitHub
  Sponsors and donations through Stripe. If you use them, their own privacy
  policies apply.
