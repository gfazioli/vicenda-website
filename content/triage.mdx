# Reading and Triage

## Read and unread

Opening a channel marks it read — after a second and a half, not instantly, so
a channel flicked past on the way to another one keeps its unread.

Marking a single message read or unread is a per-message action from the hover
toolbar. Where it goes next depends on the account and on a switch you own:

- **Gmail**, with writing enabled, sends the change to the server, so Vicenda
  and Gmail agree.
- **Without** writing enabled, the change stays local — and Vicenda says so in
  Settings rather than pretending otherwise.
- **IMAP** is the interesting case, below.

<Callout type="info">
  On an IMAP account, Vicenda keeps a small **log of intentions**. If you mark
  something read locally, that intention is remembered, and the next sync will
  not silently revert it just because the server was never told. The server
  wins again as soon as it agrees with you once.
</Callout>

## Archive and delete

Both are available on Gmail accounts where you have turned writing on. Both go
through a queue that survives the app closing, and both are checked against
what the server actually did — a write that fails does not repaint as a success.

**Neither is available on IMAP yet.** Archiving over IMAP is a copy, a flag and
an expunge, and an expunge is not reversible. That wants a design before it
wants an implementation, so the command vocabulary Vicenda speaks cannot
express it at all today.

## Two-finger triage

Swipe a row horizontally with two fingers: **right archives**, **left deletes**
— Gmail's gesture and Apple Mail's, in that order. A coloured backdrop names
the action as the row moves, and the commit is visible: the row flies out the
way you pushed it before anything happens.

A swipe on an account that cannot write **does not move at all**. A row that
stirs and stops reads as half-broken; one that plainly refuses is honest, and
the hover toolbar explains why in words.

## Reminders

Reminders are Vicenda's own idea and stay on your Mac. They never touch the
server, so they cannot collide with a flag or a label, and nothing appears in
your mailbox from another device.

## Leaving a sender

A channel that carries a `List-Unsubscribe` header offers a way out, and the
**Subscriptions** screen collects every one of them in a single list — which is
the point, since the per-channel button only reaches a sender you were already
thinking about.

Where the sender supports one-click unsubscribe, Vicenda sends a single request
and nothing else. Where it does not, it opens their page: those are different
actions with different reliability, and a row that hid the difference would
promise more than half of them can keep.

The row **stays** after you leave, marked. Senders keep sending for days, and a
row that vanished on click would read as a lie when tomorrow's issue lands.
