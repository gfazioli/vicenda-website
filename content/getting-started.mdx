# Getting Started

## Requirements

- **macOS 15** or later.
- At least one mailbox: **Gmail**, or any account reachable over **IMAP**.

Vicenda is not on the App Store. It is a free download, signed and notarised by
Apple, and it updates itself: [get it here](/download).


![Today: who is waiting on a reply, then what is unread, each row carrying the hue of the mailbox it landed in](/screenshot-today.png)

Today is what opens first, and it is deliberately not an inbox: who is waiting
on a reply comes before anything else asks for your attention, and the unread
list sits under it.

## Connecting a mailbox

Vicenda reads Gmail through Google's API and other mailboxes over IMAP. The two
behave differently in one way worth knowing up front, because it changes what
the app is allowed to do on your behalf:

| | Gmail | IMAP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading mail | yes | yes |
| Marking read and unread | yes | yes |
| Archive and delete | yes | **not yet** |
| Sending | **not yet** | **not yet** |

<Callout type="info">
  Vicenda asks for **one** Google permission, `gmail.modify`, and it is the
  narrowest one that can do the job: the next step down cannot mark a message
  read, so a message you read here would stay unread everywhere else. Google
  describes it on the consent screen as *"Read, compose, send and permanently
  delete all your email"* — that is Google's wording for the whole permission,
  not a list of what Vicenda does. Vicenda cannot send at all, and it never
  deletes permanently: it moves messages to Gmail's own Trash, where you can
  get them back.

  Whether anything is written at all is a separate, local decision in
  **Settings → Permissions**, off until you turn it on. Turning it on does not
  ask you to sign in again — the permission was granted when you connected the
  account; the switch decides whether it is used.
</Callout>

## Adding a mailbox

On first launch Vicenda opens a setup window rather than an empty inbox. Type
the address; what happens next depends on where the mailbox lives.

**Gmail.** Google's consent screen appears **over the app** — no browser opens,
and nothing listens on a port. Vicenda cannot see into that window; it receives
only Google's answer.

**Anything else** is connected over IMAP, and Vicenda asks for two things an
address cannot tell it: the server's hostname and a password. It connects on
**port 993 with TLS** and signs in **before saving anything**, so a wrong
server or a wrong password is a message on that screen rather than a broken
mailbox on your rail. The password goes straight to your keychain.

<Callout type="info">
  Some providers want an **app-specific password** rather than the one you use
  to sign in. And if the mailbox is a **Google Workspace** account — Gmail on
  your own domain — use **Use Google sign-in** on that screen instead: Google
  does not accept ordinary passwords for IMAP by default.
</Callout>

Until Google finishes verifying the app you will meet a warning screen and have
to click **Advanced → Go to Vicenda**. That is expected while verification is
pending, and it is the same for every app in that state.

Afterwards, **Settings → Accounts → Add…** connects another mailbox, or
**⇧⌘N** from anywhere.

## The first launch

The first pass is the slow one. Vicenda walks your mail, works out which
senders are machines and which are people, and builds the two populations the
sidebar is made of. After that it keeps up incrementally: new mail arrives into
the channel or thread it belongs to without a full pass.

Two things are worth expecting while it settles:

- **The sidebar is a working set, not a directory.** A label or an address that
  has seen no traffic for a fortnight is not shown by default — it has not been
  lost, and "show all" is one click under each section. Measured on a real
  mailbox: 189 labels existed and 18 had seen traffic.
- **Messages arrive before their bodies do.** Headers come first so the stream
  is usable immediately; the text follows. A row that says the body has not
  been downloaded yet means exactly that, rather than that the message is empty.

## What it will not do behind your back

- It will not **send** anything. The composer writes a local draft and says so.
- It will not **delete** anything on an IMAP server. The command vocabulary it
  speaks cannot express a delete, by construction, and there is a test that
  enumerates every command to keep it that way.
- It will not **fetch a remote resource** while you read. See
  [Privacy](/docs/privacy).
